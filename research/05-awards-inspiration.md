# 05 — Award-Winning Food Web Design Inspiration

Curated reference set for the IT — Italian Trattoria pitch. Ten sites that punch above their weight on food photography, scroll storytelling, menu UX, location UX, or counter-service that escapes the template trap. Sourced from Awwwards (Food & Drink, Restaurant, Hotel & Restaurant), Land-book, SiteInspire, and editorial design research current to 2026.

---

## Da Maria — https://damaria-roma.webflow.io
**Why it's here:** A modern Roman trattoria site that gets the warmth-vs-restraint balance exactly right. It teases dishes editorially ("Ravioli alla Nonna only until Thursday") instead of dumping a full menu wall, and a "Cook's Note" from Maria humanizes the brand in a way that no stock photo can.
**Standout technique:** Scarcity-driven dish teasers on the homepage — a rotating "today / this week only" plate with a one-line story, paired with a sticky reservation surface that follows the scroll.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The vintage-rotary-phone illustration and overtly nostalgic Roman cues — IT is a younger, counter-service energy and shouldn't cosplay as a fine-dining trattoria.

## Tenuta Centoporte — https://tenutacentoporte.it
**Why it's here:** Restraint as a flex. Almost no food photography; instead the site leads with landscape, architecture, and heritage prose ("time seems suspended between authenticity and timeless beauty"). It reads like an editorial feature, not a menu.
**Standout technique:** Place-as-protagonist opening — the first scroll act is *where you are*, not *what you eat*. Heritage typography over wide landscape plates, with progressive reveal of amenities.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The food-photography drought. IT's hero proposition is the plate; we keep the editorial pacing but bring the food forward.

## Caffè Milani — https://caffemilani.it
**Why it's here:** A 1937 Italian roaster that integrates an actual editorial "Magazine" section as a primary nav item, not an afterthought. Documentary-style heritage photography (Como, the original factory, hands at work) carries far more credibility than glossy product shots.
**Standout technique:** Magazine as a top-level nav. Seasonal stories, supplier features, and ingredient origin pieces sit next to "Menu" and "Locations," signalling the brand has things to say.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The corporate-roaster neutral palette. IT needs warmer, more saturated brand color than Milani's near-monochrome.

## CRAV Burgers — https://cravburgers.shop
**Why it's here:** The cleanest example of fast-casual that refuses to look like fast-casual. Ingredient-as-hero close-ups (tomato, cheese, meat shown as premium components), bold display type, and a global-locations narrative ("Berlin, London, NYC, Sydney, Tokyo") that turns logistics into lifestyle.
**Standout technique:** Ingredient close-ups treated as still-life photography — single ingredient, full bleed, big type — used as scroll dividers between menu sections.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The aggressive "SMASHED FRESH / BOLD FLAVOR" caps-lock voice. IT's tone is Italian neighborhood, not American-burger swagger.

## WatchHouse — https://watchhouse.com
**Why it's here:** A multi-location coffee brand with the best location/store-finder UX in the reference set. Each "House" is treated as a destination with its own feature photography and editorial card, not a row in a list.
**Standout technique:** Menu-as-identity grouping — instead of "Starters / Mains / Desserts," WatchHouse uses thematic collections ("Rituals," "Ventures," "Horizons"). For IT this maps to something like "Aperitivo / Banco / Tavola" — categories that tell guests how to eat, not just what's on offer.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The minimalist luxury palette (cream + matte black + caramel). It reads correct for premium coffee, too cold for a trattoria.

## Haven Coffee + Kitchen (Annecy) — https://haven-annecy.fr
**Why it's here:** A small French café that pairs SVG line illustrations (storefront, cup, croissant) with straight food photography. The illustrations create warmth and identity without turning the site into a kitsch coloring book.
**Standout technique:** Hand-drawn SVG marks used as section dividers and nav accents — gives the site a "designed by a human" quality that pure photography can't carry alone.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The square-grid carousel of food thumbnails — feels Instagrammy and undersells each dish. IT should give each hero dish room to breathe.

## Paput Menorca — https://paputmenorca.com
**Why it's here:** Counter-service / chiringuito energy done with point of view. A dialect tagline ("A POC A POC LIFE" — little by little) is repeated as a structural motif, and local ingredients (Mahón cheese, sobrasada, miel) are positioned as premium credentials rather than footnotes.
**Standout technique:** A short dialect or regional phrase used as a repeating typographic anchor through the scroll — a rhythm device, not just a tagline.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The graphic-illustration-instead-of-food-photography choice. Paput can get away with it because it's a beach burger shack; IT's plates deserve real photography.

## BurgerFuel — https://burgerfuel.com
**Why it's here:** A reference for vibrant, motion-forward fast-casual that still feels designed. Strong color blocking, kinetic typography, and a confident commitment to one brand voice across menu, locations, and merch.
**Standout technique:** Color-blocked section transitions where each scroll act gets its own background color tied to a menu category — a way to give a counter-service flow visual chapters.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The high-octane motion intensity. IT wants warmth and craft, not adrenaline.

## Frituur Rumbeke Platse — https://rumbekeplatse.be
**Why it's here:** The "find us" UX is the lesson here. Multiple prominent route-description buttons, transparent hours, candid owner portraits ("Jamie"), and a "Snack of the Month" feature anchored at the top and repeated at the bottom of the page.
**Standout technique:** A "dish of the week" or "today at IT" module pinned to both the top and the footer — a merchandising loop that gives returning visitors a reason to scroll all the way down.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The frituur-y nostalgia palette and SVG mascot icons. IT's identity should be more editorial.

## Wildbran — https://wildbran.pt
**Why it's here:** A Portuguese snacks brand that treats food as design object — texture-and-form photography rather than styled "appetite shots." Modular grid with full-bleed alternating sections creates strong reading rhythm.
**Standout technique:** Alternating full-bleed content blocks where every other section flips the image/text relationship — gives the scroll a magazine-spread feel without needing custom illustration.
**What IT shouldn't copy:** The packaged-product-grid mental model. IT is a restaurant; it can't be merchandised as SKUs.

## The 5 moves we should adopt

1. **Editorial type-over-image hero with a rotating "today's plate" teaser** — similar to Da Maria. Big display serif headline, full-bleed photograph of a single dish, and a one-line "only today / this week" microstory that rotates. Sets a confident editorial tone in the first viewport and immediately gives the site a reason to be revisited.

2. **Ingredient-as-still-life scroll dividers** — similar to CRAV Burgers. Between menu sections, a single ingredient gets a full-bleed, gallery-style still life (tomato, basil, olive oil, parmigiano). Turns the menu scroll into a curated tour of what IT actually cooks with, and elevates a counter-service site above template territory.

3. **Magazine as a top-level nav item** — similar to Caffè Milani. A real editorial surface — supplier features, recipe notes, neighborhood stories — sitting next to Menu and Locations. Gives IT a voice beyond the plate and a content engine for SEO and return visits.

4. **Themed menu collections instead of generic categories** — similar to WatchHouse. Replace "Starters / Mains" with named acts that tell guests how to eat at IT (e.g. "Aperitivo / Al Banco / A Tavola / Dolce"). Makes the menu feel curated rather than itemized.

5. **Sticky bottom-bar order/reserve CTA with location-aware copy** — extrapolated from CRAV's repeated CTAs and Rumbeke Platse's persistent route buttons, executed as a true mobile-first sticky bar. Default state: "Order from nearest IT." Scroll past a location section: bar updates to that location's address + hours. Single most important conversion move for a counter-service brand and the cleanest way to keep find-us friction near zero.
