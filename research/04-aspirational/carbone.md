# Carbone — Tear-down

**URL:** https://carbonenewyork.com
**Priority:** High (flagship reference)

---

## Type pairing
Custom display serif for the wordmark and section titles ("CARBONE NYC," "THE ORIGINAL") with high-contrast strokes and a vintage, slightly art-deco feel — closer to Bodoni / Didone territory than a humanist serif. Body copy is a clean, modest sans-serif used in all-caps for navigation and small UI labels. Heavy reliance on letterspaced uppercase to do hierarchy work.

## Color system
Saturated, theatrical, and tonally specific:
- Deep burgundy / wine red (banquette red, roughly `#5A1A1F` to `#7B1E2A` mood)
- Warm gold / brass accents (`#B58D3C`-ish)
- Off-black backgrounds and cream / ivory type (`#F4ECDF`-ish)
- Photography is consistently warm-lit, low-key — almost cinematic
No bright whites. Nothing modern-tech-blue. Everything reads "late-night supper club."

## Motion style
Restrained. Subtle fades and slow image swaps. No scroll-jacking, no big parallax, no kinetic type. The motion confidence comes from *not* moving — it feels like a film still, not a scroll experience.

## Hero pattern
Large editorial photography of the dining room and dishes, warm and moody, with a small centered wordmark over it. Photo-forward, type-minimal. Feels like the opening shot of a movie, not a landing page.

## Navigation pattern
Fixed top nav, centered logo, with **"RESERVE A TABLE" (Resy)** persistently parked top-right alongside "PRIVATE EVENTS" and "CONTACT CONCIERGE." Reservations is the loudest action on the entire site. No hamburger on desktop; mobile collapses cleanly.

## Menu page treatment
Image-led PDF / styled HTML hybrid. Sections like *Antipasti, Caesar, Pasta, Vongole, Veal Parmesan* are presented with theatrical naming and prices, almost no descriptions. The menu reads like a script for a meal — minimal hand-holding, maximum confidence. No per-item photography on the menu page itself; the dining room photography on the homepage does the seducing.

## Story / about page treatment
Editorial long-form scroll under "The Story." Heavy on Mario Carbone / Rich Torrisi / Jeff Zalaznick mythology, Greenwich Village context, and the "fancy red-sauce joint" thesis. Long text blocks, large pull quotes, archival-feeling photography. Reads like a *New York Times* magazine piece, not a restaurant about page.

## One thing IT should adopt
**Confidence through restraint in copy.** Carbone names a dish "Veal Parmesan" and stops. No bullet points, no "house-made," no allergen calls on the front-of-house menu. IT should let menu items stand on their own with quiet Italian phrasing and no marketing-speak — that single move makes counter-service food feel chef-driven instead of chain-deli.
