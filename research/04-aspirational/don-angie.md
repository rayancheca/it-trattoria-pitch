# Don Angie — Tear-down

**URL:** https://www.donangie.com
**Priority:** High

---

## Type pairing
A warm, slightly geometric serif wordmark (the "Don Angie" logotype itself is essentially the brand display face) paired with a neutral sans-serif (BentoBox-default-ish) for nav and body. Less typographically expressive than Carbone — they let the photography and food do the heavy lifting and keep type clean and quiet.

## Color system
Soft, warm, and West Village-cozy:
- Cream / bone background (`#F2EDE4`-ish)
- Warm charcoal text (`#1F1B16`-ish)
- Photography skews ochre, gold, copper — pasta-and-candlelight palette
- Pops of green olive and tomato red come from food, not chrome
No black-on-white starkness. Feels like a dim-lit dining room rather than a gallery.

## Motion style
Gentle. Image carousel on the hero with quiet crossfades, hover state lifts, no scroll-jacking. Motion is conservative — the brand earns its premium feel through photography and content, not animation.

## Hero pattern
Photo carousel (four slides) of interior + signature dishes — the pinwheel lasagna, the dining room — with minimal type overlay. Photography is the entire hero. Type is a quiet caption on top.

## Navigation pattern
Fixed top, horizontal, with **Reservations as a far-right CTA button (Resy / OpenTable).** Standard but well-executed: Menus, About, Shop, Reservations. Mobile collapses to hamburger. There's also a mid-page embedded reservation form — they don't make you hunt.

## Menu page treatment
Rich HTML with downloadable PDF fallback. Sections: *For the Table, Appetizers, Pasta, Mains, Sides, Desserts,* plus full drink lists with regions and vintages. Dish name in semi-bold, ingredients in lighter weight beneath, price right-aligned. Top of menu page has a five-slide food photography carousel for context — but individual items are text-only. Clean, scannable, no clutter.

## Story / about page treatment
Long-form scroll with a carousel of **candid founder portraits** of Angie Rito and Scott Tacinelli — at a fence, in front of brick walls, group photos. Intentionally un-corporate. Narrative is intimate: Angie's family bakery in Cleveland, Scott's culinary path, how they met. Reads like a couple's story, not a restaurant pitch. Big differentiator vs. Carbone's editorial polish.

## One thing IT should adopt
**Candid, un-corporate founder photography.** A counter-service trattoria can't afford and shouldn't fake the Carbone red-banquette mythology — but the Don Angie "two people who care about food, shot honestly" move is perfectly transferable. One real portrait of the owner / chef in the kitchen, slightly imperfect, beats any stock-shot polish.
