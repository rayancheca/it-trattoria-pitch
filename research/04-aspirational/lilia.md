# Lilia — Tear-down

**URL:** https://www.lilianewyork.com
**Priority:** Secondary

---

## Type pairing
Sans-serif dominant — the Lilia wordmark is itself the primary display moment. Body copy is minimal sans, prioritizing legibility. Less serif-heritage signaling than Carbone or Via Carota — Lilia is Williamsburg-modern, and the type knows it.

## Color system
Neutral high-contrast: whites, blacks, muted tones. No chrome accent. Photography (wood-fired imagery, golden pastas, Brooklyn warmth) supplies all color.

## Motion style
Subtle. Hero implements an image carousel **with explicit user-controlled play/pause** — a small but interesting design choice that respects attention rather than auto-playing. Slow, deliberate, no parallax.

## Hero pattern
Image carousel (3 slides) showing dining environment and food, with play/pause controls. Type sits quietly over imagery.

## Navigation pattern
Dual nav (main desktop + mobile toggle). Logical order: About, Menu, Reservations, Location, Gift Cards, Team. **"Team" as a top-level nav item** is notable — they put the people on the same level as the menu. Social and email as secondary.

## Menu page treatment
Clean HTML, wood-fired and pasta sections prominent. Restrained typography, no per-item photography on the menu page itself.

## Story / about page treatment
Missy Robbins-led (same chef as Misi, different room). Philosophy framed as "simple ingredients and soulful cooking, honoring regional traditions." Less credential-stacking than the Misi about page — more about the room and the ethos.

## One thing IT should adopt
**"Team" as a navigation item.** Counter-service restaurants almost never name their people. Putting a team / staff page in the top nav — with a few real names and roles — is a quiet move that makes a fast-casual room feel like a restaurant with humans in it instead of a transaction surface. Costs nothing, signals everything.
