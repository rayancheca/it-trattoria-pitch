## A. Current SERP Presence

### Query 1: "IT trattoria Miami Beach"

- **Organic results dominated by third-party platforms** rather than IT's own site. Tripadvisor, Yelp (two listings), Facebook, Lincoln Road directory, Postmates all rank ahead of or alongside `it-trattoria.com`.
- `it-trattoria.com/` and `it-trattoria.com/order/italian-trattoria` show in the top 10 but compete with their own delivery sub-routes.
- **Map Pack:** likely triggered (branded query in a clear geo) — both Miami Beach locations should appear.
- **Rich features:** Yelp review counts (722 photos / 356 reviews at Collins; 208 / 32 at Lincoln Rd) display in result snippets. No knowledge panel signal visible from the SERP fetch. Tripadvisor rating 4.3, rank #174 of 797 surfaces with rich snippet.

### Query 2: "IT trattoria NYC"

- Subdomain `restaurants.it-trattoria.com/new-york-time-square/` ranks #1 — note this is an inconsistent URL pattern vs. Miami (no parallel subdomain page for Collins or Lincoln Rd shown).
- Tripadvisor (4.4, rank #1,495 of 9,022) and Yelp dominate again. Both 7th Ave and 5th Ave locations appear.
- Seamless / DoorDash / Facebook take additional SERP real estate.
- The NYC subdomain page uses "Times Square" framing which is a stronger neighborhood-intent signal than the Miami pages.

### Query 3: "IT italian trattoria"

- The French Paris location (Yelp / Instagram `@it.trattoria.fr`) competes with US results for the brand name. This is brand cannibalization across geos with no clear US-vs-FR routing.
- Top result is `it-trattoria.com` home, then Tripadvisor NYC, then Yelp NYC, then a different Indian restaurant called "Trattoria" (Zomato) — confusion risk.

### Query 4: "italian trattoria collins ave Miami Beach"

- `restaurants.it-trattoria.com/miami/` ranks well — confirms the subdomain pattern *does* exist for Miami but is not surfacing for the broader brand query.
- Strong own-brand result. Yelp and Tripadvisor follow. Notably, `Trattoria Della Spiaggia` (now closed) still appears — opportunity to dominate semantic match on "trattoria collins".

### Query 5: "italian restaurant near Penn Station NYC"

- IT does **not** rank on page 1 despite 530 7th Ave being a 4-minute walk from Penn Station.
- Top competitors capturing this transactional intent: **Bar Primi Penn District** (Carmellini), **Alessa Cucina & Bar**, **L'Amico**, **Tony's Di Napoli**, Carmine's, Serano's, Olio e Più.
- Yelp and OpenTable category pages dominate.

### Query 6: "italian counter service Miami Beach"

- IT does **not** rank for the most distinctive functional descriptor of its own concept ("counter service Italian").
- Bar Bucce captures this query. Other results are full-service trattorias (Cecconi's, Macchialina, Osteria Positano) that are not actually counter-service — a clear SERP gap.

**Summary:** IT ranks for branded queries but leaks to aggregators (Yelp, Tripadvisor, delivery platforms) and is absent from high-intent non-branded local queries — especially "near Penn Station", "counter service", "fast casual Italian Midtown", and "Italian lunch Bryant Park."

## E. Technical SEO Baseline

### Canonical URLs

- One canonical per page. Decide: keep marketing on `it-trattoria.com` and **kill the `restaurants.it-trattoria.com` subdomain** (or 301 it into the main domain under `/locations/...`). The split currently dilutes authority and confuses Google's understanding of the US footprint.
- Trailing slash policy: enforce one (recommend trailing slash for static-feeling location pages) and 301 the other variant.
- Lowercase, hyphenated slugs only.

### hreflang for en / it / es

The US footprint should at minimum support `en-US` and `es-US` (Miami has a massive Spanish-speaking audience; NYC tourist traffic includes Spanish + Italian).

```html
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en-US" href="https://it-trattoria.com/locations/miami-beach-collins" />
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="es-US" href="https://it-trattoria.com/es/locations/miami-beach-collins" />
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="it-IT" href="https://it-trattoria.com/it/locations/miami-beach-collins" />
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default" href="https://it-trattoria.com/locations/miami-beach-collins" />
```

Coordinate with the existing French (`it.trattoria.fr` / Paris) properties via `hreflang="fr-FR"` rather than letting them compete with the US domain.

### sitemap.xml structure

Split into multiple sitemaps under a sitemap index:

```
/sitemap.xml                (index)
/sitemap-pages.xml          (home, story, catering, contact, FAQ)
/sitemap-locations.xml      (4 location pages + city hub pages)
/sitemap-menu.xml           (menu page + category pages + item pages if individual)
/sitemap-blog.xml           (editorial / story content)
/sitemap-images.xml         (image sitemap for food photography)
```

Every entry: `<loc>`, `<lastmod>`, and where relevant `<xhtml:link rel="alternate">` for hreflang.

### robots.txt

```
User-agent: *
Allow: /

Disallow: /api/
Disallow: /order/        (offload to delivery platforms — they handle their own SEO)
Disallow: /*?utm_*
Disallow: /*?ref=

Sitemap: https://it-trattoria.com/sitemap.xml
```

Reconsider whether to expose `/order/...` to indexing — if first-party ordering lives there, allow it; if it's just a passthrough to Toast/Postmates, block it.

### OG / Twitter card pattern

Per-page defaults:

```html
<meta property="og:title" content="...">
<meta property="og:description" content="...">
<meta property="og:image" content=".../og-image-1200x630.jpg">
<meta property="og:type" content="restaurant.restaurant">
<meta property="og:url" content="...">
<meta property="og:locale" content="en_US">
<meta property="og:locale:alternate" content="es_US">
<meta property="og:locale:alternate" content="it_IT">
<meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image">
<meta name="twitter:site" content="@ittrattoria">
```

Each of the 4 location pages needs its own OG image showing that specific venue, not a stock pasta shot. Each menu category needs its own.

### Image alt strategy

- Hero food shots: descriptive (`"Hand-cut tagliatelle with bolognese at IT Trattoria Lincoln Road"`) — not keyword-stuffed.
- Location exterior photos: include city + street (`"IT Trattoria storefront on Collins Avenue, Miami Beach"`).
- Staff / behind-counter photos: describe the action (`"Pizzaiolo stretching dough at IT Trattoria 5th Avenue"`).
- Decorative-only images: `alt=""` (empty) so screen readers skip them.
- Always pair with explicit `width`/`height`, modern formats (AVIF/WebP) with JPEG fallback.

### Internal linking strategy

- **Hub & spoke per city.** `/locations/miami-beach/` is the Miami hub. It links to both `/locations/miami-beach/collins/` and `/locations/miami-beach/lincoln-road/`. Same for NYC.
- Every location page links to: the menu, catering, story, the other locations in the same city, and (cross-city) the closest equivalent location.
- Menu category pages link to the locations that serve them (especially if menus differ slightly per venue).
- Story / About links to all 4 locations and to catering.
- Footer global nav lists all 4 locations with city + neighborhood (`Miami Beach — Collins Ave`, `Miami Beach — Lincoln Rd`, `NYC — Penn Station`, `NYC — Bryant Park / 5th Ave`).
- Use descriptive anchor text, not "click here" — anchor copy is one of the strongest internal ranking signals.

### Performance + Core Web Vitals targets

Per global rules: LCP < 2.5s, INP < 200ms, CLS < 0.1. Hero food imagery is the biggest LCP risk — preload one hero per location, AVIF, explicit dimensions.

### Other technical baseline items

- Single HTTPS host; HSTS preload.
- 301 every legacy URL from the old site and the subdomain.
- Self-reference canonical on every indexable page.
- 404 page that links into the location hubs.
- Cache headers: HTML short, assets immutable + hashed.
- Pagination (if blog grows) using `rel="next"`/`rel="prev"` semantics or single canonical pages.
- Verified Google Business Profile per location, fully populated, photos refreshed monthly. GBP is the single biggest local-SEO lever and lives outside the website but must be coordinated with it.
