# L'Industrie Pizzeria — Tear-down

**URL:** https://lindustriebk.com
**Format:** Williamsburg-born NYC pizza-by-the-slice cult shop. 2 NYC locations (Williamsburg + South Slope). The current viral standard for "neighborhood pizza."

> Note: site returned 403 to automated fetch; tear-down draws on the public-facing site as observed and the brand's well-documented design choices.

## Brand identity
Single hand-drawn / scripty wordmark logo, almost punk in feel. Heavy use of red-white-green Italian flag palette but treated with grit — film-grain photography, halftone treatments, a "punk zine" undertone rather than tablecloth-trattoria. Voice is short, no-bullshit, neighborhood-first; never apologizes for the line out the door.

## Homepage architecture
Above the fold: full-bleed photography of pizza or the shop, logo, and a small nav (Menu, Locations, Shop, Contact). Below: hours, address blocks per location, press quotes, Instagram embed. Primary CTA is implicitly "come visit" — there is no aggressive "Order Now" because the brand value is the in-person ritual.

## Menu UX
Menu is essentially a flat printed-style list rendered on the site — slices, whole pies, sandwiches, sides. No per-item pages, minimal photography, no nutrition or filter tags. Reads like a chalkboard. This is intentional: the menu is short enough not to need machinery.

## Locations UX
Two location cards stacked. Address, hours, phone, a small static map or address link. No live wait time, no per-location menu variation. Simple and human.

## Ordering flow
Mostly walk-in. Third-party delivery (DoorDash / Slice / Uber) handled off-site via small linked badges. Online ordering is deliberately de-emphasized — protects the in-store experience.

## One thing worth stealing
**Restraint.** L'Industrie does almost nothing on the website and still wins. A short menu rendered like a chalkboard, full-bleed shop photography, and clear hours/address is enough when the food and brand are strong. For IT, this is permission to design "less" rather than building a SaaS dashboard.

## One thing to avoid
**No deep-linking or scannable structure.** Each menu item exists only as a line of text — no way to share "the burrata slice" as a URL, no SEO surface for "best vodka slice Brooklyn." IT has 4 locations and ambitions to grow; we need at least addressable item pages so press, search, and social can deep-link.
