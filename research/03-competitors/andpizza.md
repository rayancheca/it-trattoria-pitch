# &pizza — Tear-down

**URL:** https://andpizza.com
**Format:** DC-born fast-casual oblong-pie chain, ~40 US locations.

## Brand identity
Bold black ampersand logo, condensed sans-serif (think anti-corporate, almost streetwear). Palette is black + white + a single fluorescent accent (often red or yellow). Photography is high-contrast, slightly punk, leans into "rule-breakers, risk-takers" positioning. Voice is loud, anti-corporate, identity-coded — "Pizza That Hits Different," "We Don't Do Normal."

## Homepage architecture
Above the fold: sticky nav with "Order Now," hero banner with a limited-time pie ("The Dickle Pie Is Back But Not For Long"), value-prop line. Below in order: brand mission → 3 featured pies → "We Don't Do Normal" brand-story block → location finder → vibe/culture photo strip → loyalty signup → footer. Primary CTA is "Order Now" (external, routes to Thanx).

## Menu UX
Menu items as visual cards with descriptive flavor tags ("sweet, spicy & savory," "vegan vibes"), prices upfront, "Explore Menu" link for full set. Allergen/nutrition lives on separate pages. Each pie has a name and a personality (Dickle, Maverick, etc.) — items are characters, not just products.

## Locations UX
Dedicated /locations page. Geolocation-aware "Change Location" on mobile.

## Ordering flow
Externalized to Thanx (loyalty + ordering platform). Handoff is visible but tolerable. Loyalty is heavily integrated.

## One thing worth stealing
**Named items as characters.** Each pie has a proper noun and a personality, not just an ingredient list. "The Maverick" hits harder than "Pepperoni & Hot Honey." IT can do this with at least the signature pastas — give the cacio e pepe a name, give the lasagna a name, build a small cast of dishes the regulars know by first name.

## One thing to avoid
**Volume-as-personality.** &pizza's "loud and disruptive" voice works because the brand was born in 2012 DC fast-casual culture. For IT — Italian, family-coded, regionally-rooted — copying that energy would feel like a 50-year-old wearing a Supreme hoodie. The volume has to match the product. IT should be confident, not loud.
