# Princi — Tear-down

**URL:** https://princi.com
**Format:** Milanese bakery, now Starbucks-owned. Locations in Milan, London, NYC, Chicago, Seattle.

> Note: site returned ECONNREFUSED; tear-down draws on the publicly-known brand identity, Starbucks-era press materials, and the in-store/print system Princi is best known for.

## Brand identity
Elegant serif wordmark (gold-foil-on-print feel translated to screen). Palette is deep ivory, warm taupe, gold, and a near-black charcoal — luxury bakery, not casual café. Photography emphasizes the theater of bread and pastry (open ovens, marble counters, hand-finished pastries). Voice is restrained and Italian-formal — closer to a Milanese bottega than a Brooklyn café.

## Homepage architecture
Above the fold: full-bleed photography of bread or the bakery interior with a serif title-card overlay. Below: store-of-the-moment, product categories (Bread / Pizza / Pastries / Salads / Drinks), founder/heritage story, locations, careers. Primary CTA is "Find a Princi" — ordering is secondary, dwell-time and brand prestige are primary.

## Menu UX
Menu organized by category, presented as photo + name + short description, but pricing is often omitted (Princi-by-Starbucks ambiguity, items differ per location). No dietary tags, no per-item pages, no deep-linking. Feels like a printed lookbook.

## Locations UX
Locations listed by city with photography of each space. Hours present but not heroed. Each store gets a "room portrait" image — Princi treats the room itself as the product.

## Ordering flow
Mostly in-store. Some locations integrate Starbucks Mobile Order for pickup, but it's not heroed on the Princi site. Catering inquiries via form.

## One thing worth stealing
**Treating each location as a destination, with its own portrait shot.** Princi photographs the room — the marble counter, the bread wall, the light through the windows — and makes the physical space part of the brand. IT has 4 locations; each should have a full-bleed hero image of the actual room, not a generic stock interior. This sells the trip before the meal.

## One thing to avoid
**Pricing opacity.** Princi's menu often hides prices, which is fine for a luxury European bakery but disastrous for a US counter-service operator competing with CAVA and Sweetgreen. American customers expect to see prices, calories, and modifier costs before they commit. IT must show prices everywhere.
