# Joe's Pizza NYC — Tear-down

**URL:** https://joespizzanyc.com
**Format:** 1975 Greenwich Village pizza institution. ~6 NYC locations + LA, Miami.

## Brand identity
Red-white-green stripe wordmark, hand-painted shop-sign aesthetic. Palette is the Italian flag, used unapologetically. Photography heavy on the storefront, the original Carmine Street shop, founder Pino "Joe" Pozzuoli. Voice is short, proud, neighborhood-coded — "authentic New York street slice," "Greenwich Village institution," founder still working at 75.

## Homepage architecture
Above the fold: storefront hero, logo, prominent "ORDER NOW" CTA, Instagram link, cart icon. Below: brand-story block (1975 origin, founder), location callouts, press, social. Primary CTA is "ORDER NOW" → routes to /locations (location-first ordering — you pick the shop before you pick the slice).

## Menu UX
Minimal. Menu is short by design (cheese, pepperoni, fresh mozz, a few specials). Listed flat with prices, no photography per item, no dietary tags. The product is so iconic that the menu doesn't have to sell — it just has to confirm.

## Locations UX
Locations as a routed page with cards: address, hours, phone, order link per location. Some heritage location storytelling (Bleecker & Carmine origin shop).

## Ordering flow
Per-location pickup/delivery via third-party (Toast, ChowNow, or similar — varies). The "ORDER NOW" CTA forces a location pick first, which is correct UX for a multi-location pizza shop.

## One thing worth stealing
**Location-first ordering as the default.** Clicking "ORDER NOW" doesn't dump you into a menu — it makes you pick a store first. This is the correct flow for any multi-location counter-service brand, because menu and hours differ per shop. IT must do this: pick location → then menu → then cart.

## One thing to avoid
**Founder-story-only positioning.** Joe's leans almost entirely on heritage ("since 1975"). IT is too young to do this — we don't have 50 years of Greenwich Village mythology. If we try to copy this voice, we'll sound fake. IT's voice has to be earned from something else: chef expertise, regional Italian specificity, or the food itself — not invented nostalgia.
