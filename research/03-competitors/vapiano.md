# Vapiano — Tear-down (cautionary tale)

**URL:** https://www.vapiano.com
**Format:** Global counter-service Italian chain, ~155 restaurants across 28+ countries. Bankrupted in 2020, restructured, still operating.

## Brand identity
Generic sans-serif wordmark (lowercase, friendly but anonymous). Palette is white + olive green + warm wood-tones, the visual language of a corporate "fresh casual" 2010s deck. Photography is glossy and styled — pasta on white plates with herb garnish, smiling staff, sunlit dining rooms. Voice is corporate-friendly: "Shaped by you," "unwind, eat well." Reads like the international airport food court it often is.

## Homepage architecture
Above the fold: hero with tagline "Fresh Italian" and a "Find your Vapiano" CTA, country selector. Then: about-us block, franchising callout, news/social-proof strip, contact footer. Primary CTA is the country selector — the homepage is a portal, not a destination.

## Menu UX
No menu on the corporate site at all. You have to pick a country first, which redirects to a regional site with its own menu. By the time you find pricing, you've made three navigation decisions.

## Locations UX
Country dropdown → regional site → location list. Three clicks before you see hours.

## Ordering flow
Whatever the regional site uses — totally different per country. UK has one stack, Germany another, Australia another. No unified ordering experience across the brand.

## One thing worth stealing
**Honestly, very little.** The closest thing to a useful pattern is the franchise-first navigation, which makes sense if growth-via-franchising is the main commercial goal. (For IT it is not — yet.) If we were doing aggressive expansion this would be worth studying.

## One thing to avoid
**Everything about the corporate portal model.** Vapiano is the platonic example of how a counter-service Italian brand loses its soul: a homepage that is a country selector, photography that could be any chain, no menu visible until you've made three clicks, and tagline copy that translates equally well to a bank. IT must never look like this. Every page should make it obvious this is a small, founder-rooted, regionally specific operator — not a global franchise headquarters.
