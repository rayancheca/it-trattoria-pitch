# CAVA — Tear-down (gold standard)

**URL:** https://cava.com
**Format:** Mediterranean fast-casual, ~350 US locations. Public company. The other counter-service UX benchmark.

> Note: site returned 403 to automated fetch; tear-down draws on CAVA's widely-studied public site, recent rebrand, and ordering surface.

## Brand identity
Custom serif wordmark (CAVA in distinctive ligature) paired with a clean sans-serif body. Palette is warm cream + terracotta + olive + a deep navy/black — Mediterranean in color, modern in execution. Photography is overhead bowls with bright, saturated color, hand-in-frame action shots, ingredient close-ups. Voice is warm, ingredient-forward, generous — "heart, soul, spice, beans" — sells the feeling of feeding people.

## Homepage architecture
Above the fold: full-bleed photography of a bowl being built, single headline, "Start Order" CTA, persistent location/store selector. Below: bowl-builder teaser ("Build your own"), featured items, Mediterranean-pantry/retail products (CAVA's CPG line), loyalty signup, careers, sustainability. Primary CTA is "Start Order" — funnels everything to the bowl builder.

## Menu UX
Categorized: Bowls / Pitas / Salads / Sides / Dips & Spreads / Drinks. Each item has photography, ingredient list, full nutrition, dietary tags (V vegetarian, VG vegan, GF gluten-free), and a "Start with this" CTA that pre-loads the bowl builder with that combo. Items are deep-linkable. The bowl-builder itself is a sequential builder (base → protein → toppings → dips → dressings) with running calorie totals.

## Locations UX
Map + list. Search by city or ZIP. Each location: address, hours, pickup/delivery options, dietary availability flags, and a per-location "Order" CTA. Hours and live pickup-time estimates surface upfront.

## Ordering flow
Proprietary ordering platform (web + native app), tightly integrated with loyalty. Delivery via DoorDash/Uber surfaces inside CAVA's own checkout — third-party is wrapped, not exited to.

## One thing worth stealing
**The bowl-builder as the primary CTA, not the menu.** CAVA understands customers don't want to browse 40 items — they want to assemble one bowl. "Start Order" loads the builder, not a menu list. For IT, the analog is a **pasta builder** or **plate builder**: pick base (pasta shape / focaccia / salad) → sauce → protein → topping. This is the single biggest UX lift available, and none of the Italian competitors have done it.

## One thing to avoid
**Builder-only — no editorial soul.** CAVA's site is so transaction-optimized that it has almost no editorial content, no chef voice, no provenance storytelling beyond a "sustainability" page. IT can win by being both — bowl-builder efficiency AND a regional-Italian editorial layer (pasta-of-the-week, mill of the month, sauce story). Don't let the builder eat the brand.
