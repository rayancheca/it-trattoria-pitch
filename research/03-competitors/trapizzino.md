# Trapizzino — Tear-down

**URL:** https://www.trapizzino.it
**Format:** Roman street-food chain (the pizza-pocket invented by Stefano Callegari). ~14 locations across Rome, Milan, Turin, Florence, Trieste, + New York.

## Brand identity
Logo is a stylized triangle wordmark (the trapizzino shape). Palette is warm cream + deep red + black, with a strong "Roman trattoria, but young" energy. Photography mixes product close-ups of the triangle pocket with shots of bustling counter scenes. Voice is proudly Roman, founder-driven (Callegari is named on the homepage), confident about having invented a category.

## Homepage architecture
Above the fold: hero photography of the trapizzino + the founder/category story. Then: locations list (split by city), menu highlights (classic fillings: pollo alla cacciatora, lingua in salsa verde, polpetta al sugo), supplì callout, wine program note, events/collabs, press. Primary CTA is "Find a location" — ordering online is not the goal.

## Menu UX
Flat menu, organized by category (Trapizzini / Supplì / Desserts / Kids). Each item gets a short Italian-language description and sometimes a photo, but treatment is uneven across pages. No dietary tags, no per-item pages, no filtering. Menu lives as a single scrollable page per location group.

## Locations UX
Cities listed first, then locations within each city. Each location card: address, hours, sometimes a photo. New York site is a separate subdomain with its own design language — visible inconsistency.

## Ordering flow
Walk-in primary. Some locations have delivery via local Italian aggregators (Glovo, Deliveroo) linked out. No unified ordering surface.

## One thing worth stealing
**Owning a single iconic food item visually.** Every page on the site reinforces the triangle shape — in the logo, the product photography, the section dividers. IT can do this with a single signature pasta or dish — pick one hero shape (a bowl of cacio e pepe, an arancino, whatever) and treat it as the brand's visual anchor.

## One thing to avoid
**Site fragmentation across geographies.** trapizzino.it and the New York site feel like two brands. IT has 4 US locations today — but the site needs to scale to 10–20 without splintering into per-city subsites with different fonts. One unified system, parameterized by location.
