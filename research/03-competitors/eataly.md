# Eataly — Tear-down

**URL:** https://www.eataly.com
**Format:** Italian marketplace + restaurants + e-commerce, 40+ global locations.

## Brand identity
Serif headlines (heritage-leaning, used sparingly) paired with a workhorse sans-serif for nav and product UI. Palette is neutral cream/off-white with a single deep green accent and red price-flags for sale. Voice is aspirational-but-warm — "2,000 years of agricultural history," "love at first bite" — leaning on Italian provenance over chef-personality.

## Homepage architecture
Above the fold: rotating promo banners (Sale-a-brate, Father's Day, picnic), free-shipping threshold, mega-nav, cart. Then in order: hero promos → restaurant/local-delivery callouts → 12-tile product category grid → app download for Eataly Caffè → sale carousel → pasta deep-dive editorial block → best-sellers carousel. Primary CTA is "SHOP NOW" (e-commerce first); "Book a Table" is buried in nav.

## Menu UX
Eataly is a grocery store as much as a restaurant, so "menu" = product catalog. Three-level mega-nav (Shop → Pasta → Long/Short/Filled). Restaurant menus live per-location and feel like an afterthought next to the shop. No unified item-card system across restaurant menus and e-commerce.

## Locations UX
"Stores" link in top nav goes to a list of physical marketplaces. Each store has its own subsite with restaurant pages, classes, and events — but the UX shifts noticeably between locations. No unified map view of "all Eataly restaurants near me."

## Ordering flow
Proprietary e-commerce for retail. Restaurant reservations via separate booking integration (OpenTable-style). Eataly Caffè has its own iOS/Android app for NYC pre-order. Three disconnected ordering surfaces — feels fragmented.

## One thing worth stealing
**Editorial deep-dives inside the homepage.** The "Eataly Pasta" section breaks the grid with a long-form, magazine-style block explaining provenance with photography and copy that reads like Saveur. IT can do this for pasta-of-the-week or regional Italian story arcs.

## One thing to avoid
**Fragmented ordering surfaces.** Catering, dine-in reservation, takeout, and retail all live on different platforms with different cart UIs. For a 4-location group, IT must keep one unified order surface — every fork doubles support burden and kills repeat-visit muscle memory.
